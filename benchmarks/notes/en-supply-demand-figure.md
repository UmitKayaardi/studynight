# Supply and Demand

## Demand

Demand is the quantity of a good that buyers are willing and able to buy at different prices. The law of demand states that, other things being equal, when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls. A demand curve therefore slopes downwards from left to right.

## Supply

Supply is the quantity that sellers are willing to offer for sale at different prices. The law of supply states that, other things being equal, when the price rises, the quantity supplied rises, because selling becomes more profitable. A supply curve therefore slopes upwards.

## Figure 1: Market equilibrium

Figure 1 is a graph with price on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis. A downward-sloping line labelled D (demand) crosses an upward-sloping line labelled S (supply) at a point labelled E. A dashed horizontal line from E meets the price axis at £4, labelled P*. A dashed vertical line from E meets the quantity axis at 200 units, labelled Q*. Above E, a horizontal arrow at a price of £6 is labelled "surplus". Below E, a horizontal arrow at a price of £2 is labelled "shortage".

The point where the two curves cross is the equilibrium. At the equilibrium price, the quantity buyers want to buy equals the quantity sellers want to sell. In Figure 1, the equilibrium price is £4 and the equilibrium quantity is 200 units.

## Surplus and shortage

If the price is above the equilibrium, sellers want to sell more than buyers want to buy, which creates a surplus. Sellers then tend to lower their prices. If the price is below the equilibrium, buyers want more than sellers offer, which creates a shortage, and prices tend to rise. These pressures push the market towards the equilibrium.

## Shifts in demand

A change in something other than the good's own price, such as consumers' incomes or the price of a related good, shifts the whole demand curve. If demand increases, the curve shifts to the right, and both the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity rise.
