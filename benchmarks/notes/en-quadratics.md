# Quadratic Equations

## What a quadratic equation is

A quadratic equation is an equation that can be written in the form

$$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$

where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are numbers and $a$ is not zero. The highest power of the unknown $x$ is 2. The values of $x$ that make the equation true are called its roots or solutions. A quadratic equation has at most two real roots.

## Solving by factorising

Some quadratics can be factorised into two brackets. For example, $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ can be written as $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$. If a product equals zero, at least one of the factors must be zero, so $x = 2$ or $x = 3$.

## The quadratic formula

Every quadratic equation can be solved with the quadratic formula:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

The symbol $\pm$ means that there are two calculations: one with plus and one with minus.

## The discriminant

The expression under the square root, $b^2 - 4ac$, is called the discriminant. It tells us how many real roots the equation has without solving it:

- If the discriminant is positive, there are two different real roots.
- If the discriminant is zero, there is exactly one real root, sometimes called a repeated root.
- If the discriminant is negative, there are no real roots, because a negative number has no real square root.

For example, for $x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0$, the discriminant is $2^2 - 4 \times 1 \times 5 = -16$, so this equation has no real roots.

## Graphs of quadratics

The graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ is a curve called a parabola. If $a$ is positive, the parabola opens upwards and has a lowest point. If $a$ is negative, it opens downwards and has a highest point. This turning point is called the vertex. The real roots of the equation are the $x$-coordinates of the points where the parabola crosses the $x$-axis. The vertex lies on the line $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$, which is the axis of symmetry of the parabola.
